Asphyxial
Deaths

a- without, sphyxis pulse. The Greek name promises a stopped pulse, yet the heart usually beats on for a while after breathing fails. So the word is a misnomer, and today it simply means death from want of oxygen.

breathing, then struggle, then silence

Six ideas to carry out of this chapter

Every death here ends in the same place: cells that can’t get or use oxygen. What differs is where the chain broke.
The “classic signs of asphyxia” are real findings, but not one of them is specific. The neck, the mouth, the chest and the scene make the diagnosis.
Hanging leaves an oblique mark rising to the knot. Strangulation leaves a mark that runs round. Learn why, and the rest of the comparison follows.
Only 4 to 5 kg on a ligature closes the carotid arteries, so a person can die of hanging with the feet on the floor.
In drowning, look for what only a living person could have done: breathe in water, froth, grip the weeds, carry diatoms to the marrow.
Your job ends with the findings and a reasoned opinion. Suicide, homicide or accident is finally the court’s word.

Neck lab

Most of the hanging and strangulation comparison follows from one fact: in hanging, the body’s weight pulls the ligature up towards a fixed knot. Switch between the two and watch where the mark goes.
